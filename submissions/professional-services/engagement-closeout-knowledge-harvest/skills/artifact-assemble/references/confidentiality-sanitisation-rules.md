# Confidentiality & Sanitisation Rules - Engagement Closeout
Deterministic rules consumed by `closeout_core.py` (run by `asset_identify.py`, `lesson_cluster.py`, `sanitize_check.py` and `record_check.py`). These are illustrative operating summaries; replace them with controlled firm policy and client terms before production use. Engines cite sections by number, and constants mirror this file.

## 1. Client-identifying material
### 1.1 Direct identifiers
Client names, logos, email domains, project codenames that are known outside the team, site names, account IDs and screenshots with visible tenant or workspace names are not reusable outside the engagement boundary without explicit approval. An asset that carries them is held as `HOLD_REDACTION_REQUIRED` until they are removed or approved.
### 1.2 Specific descriptors
Sector and geography descriptors may be reusable only when broad enough that they do not identify the client alone or in combination. Narrow descriptors are treated as aggregation-risk tokens (#5, `HOLD_AGGREGATION_RISK`).
### 1.3 Client-specific configuration
Configuration, routing, org-design or data artefacts built for one client (marker `client_specific_configuration`) are HELD as `HOLD_REDACTION_REQUIRED` until the client-specific content is removed, whatever the asset's reuse score.

## 2. Named individuals and personal data
### 2.1 Named people
Named individuals, personal contact details and role-plus-name references are personal data. They must be removed or replaced with role-level descriptions before any knowledge record leaves the engagement boundary; until then the item is held as `HOLD_PERSONAL_DATA_REWRITE`.
### 2.2 Blame attribution
Retrospective text that assigns fault to a named person is rewritten as a process or role-level lesson. The original wording is held inside the engagement record, not reused, and the item is held as `HOLD_PERSONAL_DATA_REWRITE` until the rewrite is reviewed.

## 3. Commercially sensitive figures
### 3.1 Sensitive figures
Pricing, contract value, revenue, margin, precise staffing/headcount, savings commitments and implementation dates are commercially sensitive when they can reveal scale, timing or terms. Use bands or remove; until then the asset is held as `HOLD_REDACTION_REQUIRED`. Exact figures may also contribute to aggregation risk.

## 4. Third-party and licensed material
### 4.1 Licensed content
Third-party diagrams, vendor screenshots, licensed research, benchmark excerpts and proprietary product configuration details require a rights check. Missing rights evidence means the asset is held as `HOLD_RIGHTS_UNKNOWN`.
### 4.2 Named third-party products
A named third-party product can be individually acceptable, but when paired with sector, geography, timing or scale it contributes to aggregation risk.

## 5. Aggregation risk
### 5.1 Combination threshold
Individually safe facts can jointly re-identify a client. Four or more distinct tokens across sector, geography, precise headcount, go-live date, named product/vendor, unique architecture, rare operating model or distinctive metric trigger aggregation review.
### 5.2 Group override
When aggregation risk is triggered, every asset participating in the combination is HELD as a group even if each asset passed its individual check. The asset's own finding moves to `HOLD_AGGREGATION_RISK` too, so no finding for that asset still reads as a release candidate.
### 5.3 Recognised aggregation tokens
The recognised tokens are `sector`, `region`, `client_headcount`, `go_live_date`, `third_party_product`, `named_vendor`, `unique_architecture`, `rare_operating_model`, `distinctive_metric`, `generic_operating_model` and `generic_training_pattern`. A token outside this list is never ignored: it counts toward the #5.1 threshold (unnamed) and holds its asset under #7.4.

## 6. Confidence and default disposition
### 6.1 Confidence floor
If the sanitisation engine confidence is below 0.80, the asset or lesson is held for human review as `HOLD_LOW_CONFIDENCE`.
### 6.2 No auto-clearance
The engine may mark an item as a release candidate for practice review, but it never clears publication or external reuse. Default disposition is HOLD until a cited rule and confidence support a narrower candidate state. An asset whose reuse score did not reach the shortlist band is held as `HOLD_NOT_SHORTLISTED`.

## 7. Clearance states
### 7.1 Candidate states
Permitted candidate states are `RELEASE_CANDIDATE_SANITISED` and `INTERNAL_REUSE_CANDIDATE`. Both require human review and retain citations.
### 7.2 Hold states
The engines emit only `RELEASE_CANDIDATE_SANITISED` as a candidate state; `INTERNAL_REUSE_CANDIDATE` is reserved for human reviewers. Hold states are `HOLD_EMBEDDED_INSTRUCTION`, `HOLD_IDENTITY_UNSCREENABLE`, `HOLD_TERMS_UNAVAILABLE`, `HOLD_UNRECOGNISED_MARKER`, `HOLD_PERSONAL_DATA_REWRITE`, `HOLD_RIGHTS_UNKNOWN`, `HOLD_REDACTION_REQUIRED`, `HOLD_AGGREGATION_RISK`, `HOLD_LOW_CONFIDENCE` and `HOLD_NOT_SHORTLISTED`. Finding targets are `asset`, `asset_group`, `lesson`, `screening`, `register` and `engagement`.
### 7.3 Precedence
When several rules apply to one asset, the asset takes the most restrictive state in the order listed in #7.2 (embedded instruction first, not-shortlisted last); every applying reason and citation is kept. De-duplication never lowers a state, and the overall disposition is `HOLD` while any hold, rewrite or escalation is open.
### 7.4 Unrecognised markers and tokens
A confidentiality marker or aggregation token the engines do not recognise holds its asset as `HOLD_UNRECOGNISED_MARKER` and escalates. The unrecognised value is never repeated.
### 7.5 Confidentiality terms unavailable
An empty `confidentiality_terms` list means client terms cannot be checked: every asset is held as `HOLD_TERMS_UNAVAILABLE`, with a `register` finding and an escalation. An absent or non-list `confidentiality_terms` is an input error.
### 7.6 Lesson findings
Each retrospective input that names a person, assigns blame or names the client, and each decision that names the client, gets a `lesson` finding with disposition `rewrite`. Its clusters are marked `rewrite_required`, and the knowledge record lists the evidence ID as held without repeating the source text.

## 8. Embedded instructions and screening
### 8.1 Embedded-instruction screen
Every supplied free-text value (engagement, artifact, asset, decision, retrospective and terms fields, list items and upstream escalations) is screened before any rule reads it, through the shared `steering_guard.py` detector with the closeout hook vocabulary. Each value is screened line by line, then in adjacent line pairs, then with the other fields of its record (the whole record and each pair of its fields, two list items only as neighbours in one list), with the adjacent record of the same list (both records together and each field of one with each field of the other, list items only across the boundary), and as runs of adjacent list items (windows of two and three items and overlapping blocks of 16). IDs, marker tokens and sources are screened with `-`, `_`, `.` and `:` read as spaces and are refused on a hit; an ID is also refused when a segment spells a clause word or an imperative or verdict verb (§8.6). Screening is linear in the number of records and list items, and its ceilings only keep one run inside its time budget: each input array and the upstream `escalations` list holds at most 1000 records, the input arrays and `escalations` together at most 2500, and each text or name list inside a record (`named_people`, `third_party_products`) at most 200 items. A larger input is refused as an input error before anything is screened (value not repeated), so nothing is dropped silently; harvest one workstream or phase per run, each with its own knowledge record. Expect about 9 seconds per stage for 100 candidate assets, about 35 for 400 and about 90 for 1000, and up to about 2.5 minutes per stage at the 2500-record ceiling. Each distinct text reaches the detector once per run, and its result is reused wherever the text joins a record, a pair or a run; the joins report exactly what the detector's field join reports. A value that trips, or is too long to screen, is replaced with the withheld placeholder and gets a `screening_holds` entry (path, location, detector family, reason, citation); its text is never repeated, never read by a rule and never classified. An asset with withheld text is held as `HOLD_EMBEDDED_INSTRUCTION`; withheld evidence clusters as `unclassified` with an `embedded_instruction` signal.
### 8.2 Rendered-document screen
Before `sanitize_check.py` returns, the knowledge-record skeleton is rendered and screened line by line and in adjacent-line pairs. A hit withholds every supplied value that contributes to those lines (hold location `rendered`), the asset or evidence that carried them is held as `HOLD_EMBEDDED_INSTRUCTION`, and the whole chain is derived again until no rendered line trips. The same screen reads rendered titles and citations with their neighbours in rendering order (§8.6); those holds have the family `render_shape` and cite #8.6.
### 8.3 Client identity
The client name is harvested from the unmodified input before any screening. If the client name itself is withheld, every other field that repeats it or its distinctive first word is withheld as `client_identity`, every asset is held as `HOLD_IDENTITY_UNSCREENABLE`, and the knowledge record gets no drafted context or lesson text.
### 8.4 Supplied markers and placeholders
Input text shaped like a withheld marker is held as `supplied_marker`; it never suppresses a rule. In an intermediate, the exact withheld placeholder is accepted only at a path that carries its own screening hold; an unbound placeholder is an input error.
### 8.5 Knowledge-record draft check
`record_check.py` re-derives the governed payload, writes the deterministic skeleton and checks a filled draft. Every line except the context and lesson slots must match the skeleton exactly. Each slot holds one line of generalized prose and is refused for: embedded instructions (alone or with the next line), withheld markers or slot brackets, the client name or descriptor, a sector-and-region pair, dates, the headcount, third-party products, named people, held asset or evidence IDs and held titles, copied stage guidance, or copied source wording (any six-word run of a longer source value, or the whole normalised phrase of a source value under six words; single IDs and structural tokens such as `n/a` or `none` are ignored).
### 8.6 Render policy
Supplied titles and citations are rendered into the governed record and the skeleton, so they render only in a positive allow-shape, whether or not the detector trips. A title is a short noun phrase (at most 10 words and 80 characters) with no clause word, no imperative or verdict verb and no verdict phrase such as "cleared for" or "as is". A citation is a source path or document ID (no spaces; it carries a `.`, `/`, `\` or a digit) with an optional `#anchor` and row, line, clause, section, page or named-sheet locators, `;`-separated, at most 160 characters; no path segment, anchor or sheet name may spell a clause word or verb, and the citation's words read in their rendered order may not spell one either: every source, anchor and sheet name of all its parts in order, and each contiguous boundary between adjacent `;` parts, read by the boundary recogniser of (8) (a source with the next source, the last anchor or sheet name of one part with the first of the next), on every record that carries a citation, titled or not (`D0C-Publi#shThisRecord`, `D0C-Publi sheet shThisRecord`, `DOC-1#Publi; DOC-2#shThisRecord`, `DOC-4#Publi; shThisRecord-7.md`). Only contiguous boundaries are read, so a source segment is not paired with an anchor further along (`raw/set-up-guide.md#all-records` renders). Titles, IDs, citation paths, anchors and sheet or tab names all pass one canonical token check. (1) Any Unicode format (Cf) character, such as a zero-width space, fails the shape; it is never stripped, and the value is never validated as one copy and rendered as another. (2) The other tests read comparison skeletons that are never rendered: NFKD with combining marks (Mn) dropped, `@` read as `a` and `$` as `s`, and, inside a run of letters and digits that holds a letter, the look-alike digits 0, 3, 4, 5 and 7 read as o, e, a, s and t and 1 as i in one copy and l in the other (`publ1sh`, `Publísh`, `$kip`); plain numbers such as `Phase 1` or `AST-001` are unchanged. (3) No separator token (whitespace in a title; `-`, `_`, `.`, `/` or `\` elsewhere) may be a clause word (an all-capitals acronym such as `IT` excepted) or end in n't. (4) The value is split into parts at every other non-alphanumeric character and at case and digit boundaries (`PublishThisRecord`, `APIGateway`); the parts in order may not spell a verdict or clause phrase, and the steering words already, exactly, kindly, please, nothing or nobody and verdict phrases of five or more letters with their spaces removed count anywhere inside one run-together part (`pleaseapprove`, `approveforexternaluse`, `theyare`) and across parts only when they start at a part start and end at a part end (`PleaseApprove`, `Plea-se review`), so `Roadmap lease review` and `raw/roadmap-lease.md` render. A verdict phrase also counts, wherever its ends fall and even when its first word begins mid-token, when a case transition inside a run of letters falls on one of its own word boundaries, because that transition is the evidence of a phrase (`xsafeToReuse`, `ItemnoONENEEDS`); a space is no such evidence, so `Safe Tomorrow plan` and `Casino One playbook` render, while the run-together `SafeTomorrow` and `CasinoOne` are withheld. (5) Verbs match at lexical boundaries or before clause material, not as raw substrings. An imperative or verdict verb (publish, approve, skip, waive, consider and the like) is a clause when it fills whole parts (`Publish`, `APPROVE`, `Pub|Lish`), or when a verb of five or more letters starts a part and runs on into letters that are not just an inflection or derivational ending (s, es, d, ed, ing, r, er, ers, ation, able, al, ment, ance: `publishthisrecord`, `PUBLISHKNOWLEDGEBASE`). Anywhere else, and for every lead verb that is also a noun (release, share, mark, review, record and the like), the verb is a clause only when clause material follows it, directly or after such an ending: the next part is a determiner, pronoun or `now` (the, this, these, that, those, it, its, them, all, every, any, each, our, their, my, his, her, us, me, you, your, everything), or for an imperative or verdict verb also record, asset or a preposition (for, to, as, without); or the next part begins with one or more determiners and then a known object, whatever follows (`ItempublishTHISRECORDNOW`, `ReleaseTHISRECORDNOW`; `Review Allocations` and `Release Theme` render); or, inside one run of letters, the next letters begin the, this, these, that, those, them, their, all, every, each, any, our, now, record or asset (`skipthisrecord`, `ReleaseThisRecord`, `PublishingThisRecordNow`). A lead verb shorter than four letters (`use`, `set`, `add`, `put`, `let`, `go`) counts only at the start of a part, and only when everything after it, up to a part boundary, is clause material in full: one or more determiners (the, this, these, that, those, all, every, each, any, our, their, my, your, its) and then an object (record, asset, entry, item, section, file, document, page or lesson, singular or plural), or the pronoun it, them or everything, optionally followed by `now` (`usethisrecord`, `AST-gothisrecord`, `raw/setthisrecord.md`, `useit`, `Usethis Record`); a short verb that fills a whole part before a determiner part is a clause as before (`Set all records`). So `Gothenburg`, `Setallocations`, `Usecase`, `Lethbridge`, `user research record`, `settlement record` and `address book` stay names and noun phrases. So a derived noun or a verb inside a longer word stays a noun phrase (`Reconsideration log`, `Unauthorized access review`, `Publishing guidelines`, `Approver matrix`, `warehouse`), and a noun reading applies only while the whole value stays noun-shaped. (6) A negation (`do not`, `does not`, `did not`, run together) is a clause only when a verb or clause material follows it (`DoNotPublishThis`), so an established name such as `DoNotDisturb` renders. (7) The title and citation of one record (engagement, artifact, candidate asset) render together, so the check without the separator-token test also reads the title followed by the citation's path segments, anchors and sheet names, the reverse order, and, because a directory prefix such as `raw/` is conventional, the title next to each source's file name (extension dropped) in either order, and the title next to each anchor and sheet name of every `;` part at their touching ends (title then name, and name then title). All these joins use the boundary recogniser of (8) (`Publi` with `DOC-1#shThisRecord` or `DOC-1#intro; DOC-2#shThisRecord`, `Set` with `DOC-1#thisrecordpublic`, and `Release` with `DOC-3#all-records` are withheld; `Go-live readiness review` with `raw/go-live-runbook.md#all-sites`, `Data set` with `DOC-3#all-records` and `Roadmap` with `DOC-1#lease-accounting` render); a clause split between them (`Publi` + `raw/shThisRecordNow.md`) withholds both fields with a `render_shape` hold (location `record`), and a half held only because it spells a clause, though otherwise identifier-shaped, still joins and withholds the other (a half held for any other reason stays out of the join). (8) Every boundary join (a title with its citation, the words of a citation in order, adjacent `;` parts, neighbour records) uses one token-anchored boundary recogniser that reads only the touching ends. A word that straddles the boundary, each side giving at least one letter, counts only when its left piece starts at a token start in the left value (a word boundary, punctuation, or a case or digit transition) and its right piece ends at a token end in the right value: `Roadma|p` with `lease-accounting` is no word, since `p` starts no token, nor is `Sk|ip` with `ipad-guide`, since `ip` ends no token. A steering word or verdict phrase anchored this way is a clause. A trigger verb split this way is a clause whenever another token follows it, and an imperative or verdict verb even when none does, because a verb broken across two fields is itself the signal (`Sk` with `DOC-1#ipValidation`, `Publi` with `DOC-1#shInstructions`); if its right piece runs on inside one token, clause material must follow in full (`Publi` with `DOC-1#shthisrecord`). A whole verb that ends the left value and starts a part of it is a clause when the right value begins with one or more determiners and then a known object, or with it, them or everything, whatever follows (`Set` with `DOC-1#thisrecordpublic`; `Set` with `DOC-1#items-list` or `DOC-1#theme-guide` renders); a lead verb that is also a noun counts so only when it also starts the left value, since the last word of a longer noun phrase is its head noun. A negation that ends the left value counts likewise before clause material. Neighbour records of one input list render one after the other, so each rendered title or citation of a record is read with each of the next record's, as adjacent `;` parts are (`DOC-1#Publi` then `DOC-2#shThisRecord` on two decision-log entries); a hit withholds both with a `render_shape` hold (location `run`). Govern then renders values side by side that were not input neighbours, so the rendered-document screen (§8.2) also reads them in rendering order: the titles of released assets in table order, where a title withheld here takes its asset out of the table and its new neighbours are read at once (`Publi`, a held asset, `shThisRecord`), and the lesson evidence citations in line order across lessons. A hit withholds both with a `render_shape` hold (location `rendered`, citing #8.6), and the chain is re-run until nothing new is found, so a withholding that creates a new adjacency is read too; the knowledge-record check re-derives the governed record, so a later stage cannot introduce an unscreened adjacency. List items (named people, products, tokens) never render, so they need no join. Known limits: look-alike letters from other scripts are not folded in titles (IDs and citations are ASCII only), a clause split between an ID and a title, across records that are not neighbours in one list or in rendering order, or between pieces that do not render next to each other (a title and a segment inside a document ID, `Publi` with `D0C-shThisRecord`) is not joined; an anchor or sheet name made of clause words (`raw/release-notes.md#this-record`) is withheld, and so is a trigger verb whose halves are whole tokens on both sides of a boundary with another token after it (a title ending in the token `Rel` with `#ease-notes`). A failing ID is refused as an input error (§8.1). Any other value is replaced with the withheld placeholder and gets a `screening_holds` entry (location `field`, family `render_shape`); its text is never repeated. The record keeps the field: a withheld asset title is flagged `title_withheld: true` and the asset is held as `HOLD_EMBEDDED_INSTRUCTION`; a lesson with a withheld citation is flagged `citation_withheld: true`; the skeleton shows `title_withheld` or `citation_withheld` with the record ID in place of the text.
